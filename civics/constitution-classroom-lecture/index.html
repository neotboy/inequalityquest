Who has the power?

The United States is a republic

  • Americans vote for REPRESENTATIVES—leaders who make laws for them.
  • Congress is made of elected representatives in the House and Senate.
  • In a DIRECT democracy, voters would decide each law themselves.
EXAMPLE: Iowa voters elect members of Congress instead of voting on every federal law.

“We the People”

Popular sovereignty

  • POPULAR SOVEREIGNTY means the people are the source of government power.
  • Leaders have authority because the people choose a government—not because a king owns the country.
  • Citizens can vote leaders out of office at the next election.
THINK: Why is an election different from a king passing power to his child?

Why write a Constitution?

The country's main set of rules

  • The Constitution SETS UP the government and its three branches.
  • It gives each branch POWERS and places LIMITS on them.
  • It protects RIGHTS the government must respect.
REAL LIFE: Congress can write laws, but it cannot make a law that violates the First Amendment.

What is the highest law?

Supremacy of the Constitution

  • The U.S. Constitution is the SUPREME LAW of the country.
  • Federal and state officials must obey the Constitution.
  • A court can block a law when it violates the Constitution.
REAL LIFE: A state cannot enforce a law that violates a right protected by the U.S. Constitution.

Can the rules change?

Amendments

  • An AMENDMENT is an official change or addition to the Constitution.
  • The Constitution has 27 amendments.
  • Changes are difficult so that major rules cannot be rewritten easily.
REAL HISTORY: The 19th Amendment banned denying the vote based on sex.

How do amendments pass?

Two steps to remember

  • Usually, TWO-THIRDS of both houses of Congress must propose an amendment.
  • Then THREE-FOURTHS of the states must ratify, or approve, it.
  • The president does NOT sign constitutional amendments.
KEY NUMBERS: 2/3 PROPOSES → 3/4 APPROVES.

What is the Bill of Rights?

The first 10 amendments

  • The BILL OF RIGHTS is the first TEN amendments to the Constitution.
  • The states ratified these amendments in 1791.
  • They protect freedoms and legal rights, and limit government power.
REAL LIFE: A person accused of a crime has rights to fair legal procedures.

Your five freedoms

The First Amendment

  • SPEECH: express opinions. RELIGION: practice a faith or none. PRESS: publish news and opinions.
  • ASSEMBLY: gather peacefully. PETITION: ask government to change something.
  • These rights have legal limits; threats and major school disruptions are not automatically protected.
EXAMPLE: Write a letter to the school board, publish criticism, or peacefully protest a policy.

Why did the colonies leave?

The Declaration of Independence

  • The colonies were ruled by Great Britain and argued that government ignored their rights.
  • In 1776, the colonies approved the DECLARATION OF INDEPENDENCE.
  • THOMAS JEFFERSON was its main writer.
HISTORY: The Declaration announced the colonies' decision to separate from Britain.

Two documents, two jobs

Declaration vs. Constitution

  • DECLARATION OF INDEPENDENCE: explains WHY the colonies became independent.
  • U.S. CONSTITUTION: explains HOW the new national government is organized.
  • The Declaration came FIRST, before the Constitution was written.
REMEMBER: DECLARATION = WHY WE LEFT. CONSTITUTION = HOW WE GOVERN.

What rights did they claim?

Natural rights

  • The Declaration describes LIFE, LIBERTY, and the PURSUIT OF HAPPINESS.
  • These are NATURAL RIGHTS: rights people have simply because they are people.
  • In 1776, enslaved people, women, and others were denied many of these rights in real life.
DISCUSS: Can a country claim people are equal while its laws treat them unequally?

Four dates that matter

Put the founding events in order

  • 1776 — Declaration of Independence approved.
  • 1787 — Constitution written. 1788 — Constitution ratified.
  • 1791 — Bill of Rights ratified.
TIMELINE: DECLARATION → CONSTITUTION → APPROVAL → BILL OF RIGHTS.

Who decides what gets sold?

Market economy

  • In a MARKET ECONOMY, private businesses sell goods and services.
  • Customers choose what to buy; businesses compete for their money.
  • Prices can change when supply, demand, or competition changes.
REAL LIFE: Two sneaker shops sell the same shoes. One lowers its price to win customers.

Does the president obey laws?

Rule of law

  • RULE OF LAW means everybody must follow the law.
  • It applies to everyday people, judges, mayors, and presidents.
  • Officials cannot use government power as a personal favor.
REAL LIFE: A mayor cannot legally cancel parking tickets only for friends.

What shaped U.S. government?

Early documents and ideas

  • MAYFLOWER COMPACT: settlers agreed to make rules and govern together.
  • ARTICLES OF CONFEDERATION: first national system; Congress had too little power to fix problems.
  • FEDERALIST PAPERS: essays arguing that states should approve the new Constitution.
REMEMBER EACH PURPOSE—not just the three titles.
