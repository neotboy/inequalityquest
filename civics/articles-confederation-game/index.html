YOUR FIRST NATIONAL GOVERNMENT

The rules you’re stuck with.

After fighting British rule, many Americans feared giving another central government too much power.

Real rules. Invented missions.

The Articles took effect in 1781. They created a national Congress while leaving most power with the states.

Congress could conduct diplomacy, make treaties, and borrow money. It relied on states to raise taxes for national expenses and lacked power to regulate interstate commerce. Important decisions, including borrowing money, required nine state votes. Changes to the Articles required all thirteen states’ approval.

The missions simplify these problems. Their numbers, replies, negotiations, and outcomes are game examples, not accounts of specific historical votes.

In 1787, delegates met to revise the Articles and ended up proposing a new Constitution with a stronger national government. Adoption and the new government’s start came later.

POWER NOT INCLUDED

You can ask.
You can’t collect a national tax.

Under the Articles, Congress relied on the states to raise money. A request did not guarantee payment.

POWER NOT INCLUDED

Congress can’t make one national trade rule.

Congress lacked power to regulate trade among states. You can negotiate cooperation, but you cannot simply order the barriers away.
