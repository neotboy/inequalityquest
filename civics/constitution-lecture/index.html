01 / 14WHO MAKES THE LAWS?

A REPUBLIC

We vote for LEADERS. Those leaders vote on laws for us.
IOWA EXAMPLE

Iowans elect U.S. senators and representatives. They vote in Congress.

GAME CLUE

Republic ≠ everybody voting on every law.

02 / 14WHERE DOES POWER COME FROM?

WE THE PEOPLE

POPULAR SOVEREIGNTY means government gets its power from the people.
REAL LIFE

Voters can replace a leader during an election. A king does not need to win an election.

GAME CLUE

Think: WHO gives the government its power?

03 / 14WHAT IS THE CONSTITUTION?

THE MAIN RULEBOOK

It SETS UP government, GIVES AND LIMITS power, and PROTECTS rights.
REAL LIFE

Congress may pass laws—but those laws cannot take away rights protected by the Constitution.

GAME CLUE

Remember all THREE jobs of the Constitution.

04 / 14WHAT IF STATE AND FEDERAL RULES CLASH?

THE HIGHEST LAW

The U.S. CONSTITUTION is the SUPREME LAW of the country.
REAL LIFE

A state cannot enforce a law that breaks a right guaranteed by the U.S. Constitution.

GAME CLUE

A state law cannot overrule the Constitution.

05 / 14CAN THE CONSTITUTION CHANGE?

AMENDMENTS

An AMENDMENT adds to or changes the Constitution. It has been amended 27 times.
HOW IT USUALLY HAPPENS

2/3 of the House and Senate propose → 3/4 of states approve.

GAME CLUE

Know both the word AMENDMENT and the two fractions.

06 / 14WHAT ARE THE FIRST TEN?

BILL OF RIGHTS

The BILL OF RIGHTS is the first TEN amendments. Ratified in 1791.
REAL LIFE

People accused of crimes have important legal protections. Government must follow fair procedures.

GAME CLUE

Bill of Rights is PART of the Constitution, not the Declaration.

07 / 14FIRST AMENDMENT: FIVE FREEDOMS

KNOW ALL FIVE

SPEECH · RELIGION · PRESS · PEACEFUL ASSEMBLY · PETITION.
REAL LIFE

Write an opinion article, attend a peaceful protest, or ask a city council to change a law.

GAME CLUE

Peaceful assembly means gathering. Petition means asking government to act.

08 / 14WHY DID THE COLONIES LEAVE BRITAIN?

INDEPENDENCE: 1776

The DECLARATION OF INDEPENDENCE explained why the colonies broke away from Britain.
WHO WROTE IT?

THOMAS JEFFERSON was the main writer. Congress approved it in 1776.

GAME CLUE

DECLARATION = WHY. CONSTITUTION = HOW.

09 / 14WHAT RIGHTS DID THEY CLAIM?

NATURAL RIGHTS

The Declaration says people have rights to LIFE, LIBERTY, and the PURSUIT OF HAPPINESS.
REAL HISTORY

The words spoke of equality, but many people—especially enslaved people—were denied those rights.

GAME CLUE

These were ideas in the DECLARATION, not a list of branches.

10 / 14PUT THE DOCUMENTS IN ORDER

FOUR DATES

1776 — DECLARATION. 1787 — CONSTITUTION WRITTEN.
THEN WHAT?

1788 — CONSTITUTION RATIFIED. 1791 — BILL OF RIGHTS RATIFIED.

GAME CLUE

Remember the sequence, not just one date.

11 / 14WHO CHOOSES WHAT TO BUY?

MARKET ECONOMY

PRIVATE BUSINESSES sell goods. CUSTOMERS choose. COMPETITION changes prices.
REAL LIFE

Two stores sell the same sneakers. If one charges $140 and another $100, shoppers may choose the cheaper store.

GAME CLUE

Think: buyers, sellers, choices and competition.

12 / 14CAN THE PRESIDENT BREAK THE LAW?

RULE OF LAW

NO ONE IS ABOVE THE LAW. Presidents, mayors and everyday people must obey it.
REAL LIFE

A mayor cannot legally erase friends’ traffic fines just because they are friends.

GAME CLUE

Rule of law ≠ leaders can do whatever they want.

13 / 14WHAT CAME BEFORE THE CONSTITUTION?

EARLY GOVERNMENT IDEAS

MAYFLOWER COMPACT: People agreed to govern together. ARTICLES OF CONFEDERATION: The first national government was too weak.
AFTER 1787

FEDERALIST PAPERS: Essays that argued for approving the new Constitution.

GAME CLUE

Know which document did WHAT.
