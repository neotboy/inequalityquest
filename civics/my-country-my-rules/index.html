HISTORY BRIEFING · OUTSIDE THE GAME

Real people. Real harm.

The lesson also studies crimes against humanity by communist regimes since 1917. Crimes against humanity are serious attacks on civilians, such as murder, forced labor (people made to work), or persecution (harm aimed at a group). These events are history, not game choices.

Soviet Union under Stalin

During the Great Terror in the 1930s, Stalin’s government arrested and killed many people. It sent many others to forced-labor camps, where people had to work. Some people were punished because leaders called them enemies of the state.

Source: Library of Congress ↗

Cambodia, 1975–1979

The communist Khmer Rouge regime forced people to leave cities and work. It targeted groups and carried out mass killings. Its rule caused great suffering and death.

Source: U.S. Holocaust Memorial Museum ↗

A regime may say one thing and do another. Look at who had power, how leaders treated people who disagreed, and what happened to people.
