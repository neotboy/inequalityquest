The most common mistake on these questions is mixing up the Declaration and the Constitution. The Declaration (1776) came before the Constitution (1787). Point out the real documents: "In Congress, July 4, 1776" at the top of the Declaration and the giant "We the People" at the top of the Constitution. Colors: gold is the Declaration, blue is the Constitution, red is the Bill of Rights.

BIG IDEA 1 · THE RULEBOOK

The Constitution is the rulebook

TEST QUESTION 2 ★

What is the supreme law of the land?

(U.S.) Constitution

“Supreme” means highest. No law can go against it.

TEST QUESTION 3

Name one thing the U.S. Constitution does.

  • Forms the government
  • Defines powers of government
  • Defines the parts of government
  • Protects the rights of the people

WATCH: The making of the American Constitution

Video for this section: "The making of the American Constitution" (link at the bottom of the slide). The photo is the real Constitution. "We the People" is written huge on purpose, because it says the power comes from the people. Think of the Constitution like the rulebook for a game. It says who the players are (the parts of government), what each one can do (powers), and what they can't do to you (rights). Q3 has four right answers, and the test could use any of them.

BIG IDEA 1 · THE RULEBOOK

Changing the rulebook

TEST QUESTION 5

How are changes made to the U.S. Constitution?

  • Amendments
  • The amendment process

An amendment is a change or addition.

TEST QUESTION 7 ★

How many amendments does the U.S. Constitution have?

27

Twenty-seven (27)

TEST QUESTION 6

What does the Bill of Rights protect?

  • (The basic) rights of Americans
  • (The basic) rights of people living in the United States

Bill of Rights = the first 10 of the 27 amendments (1791).

WATCH: Why wasn’t the Bill of Rights originally in the US Constitution?

Video for this section: "Why wasn't the Bill of Rights originally in the US Constitution?" (link at the bottom). Watch for the trap: 10 is the Bill of Rights, but the total is 27. Point out the parentheses in the answers. "(The basic) rights of Americans" means "rights of Americans" also counts. Ask: the Bill of Rights protects people's rights. Whose power does it limit? (The government's.)

The Declaration is the breakup letter

TEST QUESTION 11

“Life, Liberty and the pursuit of Happiness”

is in the Declaration of Independence

TEST QUESTION 9

What founding document said the American colonies were free from Britain?

Declaration of Independence

TEST QUESTION 8

Why is the Declaration of Independence important?

  • It says America is free from British control.
  • It says all people are created equal.
  • It identifies inherent rights.
  • It identifies individual freedoms.

WATCH: What you might not know about the Declaration of Independence

Video for this section: "What you might not know about the Declaration of Independence" (link at the bottom). The photo is the top of the real Declaration: "In Congress, July 4, 1776." Breakup-letter idea: the Declaration says "we're leaving, and here's why." It lists complaints against King George III and says people have rights the government can't take away. "Inherent" rights = rights you're born with. It does NOT set up the government. That's the Constitution's job 11 years later. Q8, Q9, and Q11 all point to the Declaration.

BIG IDEA 3 · THE PEOPLE ARE IN CHARGE

The people are in charge

TEST QUESTION 1

What is the form of government of the United States?

  • Republic
  • Constitution-based federal republic
  • Representative democracy

Any ONE of these counts.

TEST QUESTION 4

The Constitution starts with “We the People.” What does “We the People” mean?

  • Self-government
  • Popular sovereignty
  • Consent of the governed
  • People should govern themselves
  • (Example of) social contract

Photos: Wikimedia Commons (vote sign; early American ballot box)

Point to the photos: voting is how the people stay in charge. The wooden box is an old ballot box that used small balls instead of paper ballots. Q4 is where students lose points. "The people give the government its power" is the right idea, but the test uses these official words. The next slide breaks down what each one means. Q1: a republic means we elect representatives. We don't vote on every law ourselves.

Key words in plain English

WordWhat it meansExample
Popular sovereigntyThe people hold the powerVoters choose who leads
Consent of the governedLeaders only have power because the people agree to itA mayor who loses the election has to leave
Self-governmentPeople make their own rules, not a kingCitizens elect a city council
Social contractPeople give up some freedom in exchange for safety and orderYou follow traffic laws so the roads are safe

All four of these are accepted answers for test question 4.

Have students pick one word and explain it to a partner in their own words using a new example. Listen for: power comes FROM the people. Social contract also shows up as an answer for Q10.

Q10: Pick any TWO of these ideas

Name two important ideas from the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution.

Equality

Everyone is treated the same under the law

Liberty

Freedom to live your life and make choices

Social contract

Give up some freedom for safety and order

Natural rights

Rights you’re born with, like life and liberty

Limited government

Government can only do what the Constitution allows

Self-government

People make their own rules instead of a king

This is the only question where students need TWO answers. Picture links: the Supreme Court building has "Equal Justice Under Law" carved on the front (equality). The Liberty Bell stands for liberty. Voting shows the social contract: we agree to rules and choose leaders. The Declaration names natural rights. The Constitution limits government. The ballot box stands for self-government. Easy pair to remember: "liberty and equality." Wrong answers to watch for: "divine right of kings," "loyalty to Britain," "unlimited government."

Q15: Why three branches?

LEGISLATIVE

Congress

Makes the laws

EXECUTIVE

President

Carries out the laws

JUDICIAL

Courts

Decide what laws mean

ACCEPTED ANSWERS · ANY ONE COUNTS

So one part does not become too powerful · Checks and balances · Separation of powers

Example of checks and balances: the President vetoes a bill, and two-thirds of Congress votes to override it.

Each branch has a real building: the Capitol (Congress), the White House (President), and the Supreme Court. Separation of powers = power is split into three parts. Checks and balances = each part can stop the others. Use the veto example in the footer. Ask: what could go wrong if one person made the laws, enforced them, AND judged them?

BIG IDEA 4 · NO ONE GETS TOO MUCH POWER

Q13: What is the rule of law?

ACCEPTED ANSWERS

  • Everyone must follow the law.
  • Leaders must obey the law.
  • Government must obey the law.
  • No one is above the law.

CASE FILE

A U.S. senator is caught taking bribes. She is arrested and put on trial, just like anyone else would be.

That’s the rule of law. Being powerful doesn’t get you a free pass.

Founders’ Files · Iowa Civics Test, Section 1

Turn-and-talk: think of a time someone with power got treated like the rules didn't apply to them. That could be a coach, a celebrity, or anyone else. Keep it general, no names of classmates. Then flip it: what would school be like if the rules only applied to students and never to staff? This same case shows up in the game.

Students only need one, so the easiest are the Declaration of Independence or the Articles of Confederation. They should still RECOGNIZE the others on a multiple-choice test. BIG IDEA 5 · THE ECONOMY

Q12 ★ What is the economic system of the United States?

ANSWER

Capitalism

ALSO ACCEPTED

Free market economy

What it means: most businesses are owned by private people, not the government. Prices are mostly set by buying and selling.

Founders’ Files · Iowa Civics Test, Section 1

Keep it factual: this question asks what the U.S. system is called. Quick example: a local pizza place is owned by a person, not the government, and it sets its own prices based on what customers will pay. Wrong choices students may see: socialism, communism, feudalism.

Trap: the answer can look different

How to beat it

  • Learn to recognize EVERY answer on your study sheet, not just one.
  • Learn the ★ questions first: Q2, Q7, and Q12.
  • Wrong choices usually come from the wrong document or the wrong time period.
