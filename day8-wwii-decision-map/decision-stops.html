What do the choices mean? Negotiate = try to make a deal. Sanctions = limit trade to pressure a country. Defend allies = help a country you promised to protect. Declare war = officially join the fighting.

Four decision stops

1. Germany attacks Poland · September 1939

Germany invaded Poland. Britain and France had promised to protect Poland. If they kept that promise, they could enter a wider war.

History: Britain and France declared war on Germany on September 3. They could not stop Poland from being conquered. The war had begun in Europe.

2. France falls · June 1940

Blitzkrieg means “lightning war.” German planes and tanks attacked quickly. France surrendered. Britain was still fighting Germany, and German leaders were considering an invasion of Britain.

History: Britain's new prime minister, Winston Churchill, chose to keep fighting. Germany bombed Britain, but could not force it to surrender.

3. Japan expands in Asia · 1937–1941

Japan invaded parts of China and moved into Southeast Asia. Japan wanted land and supplies. The United States wanted Japan to stop, but the countries were not at war yet.

History: The United States stopped selling Japan oil and froze Japanese money. The U.S. kept negotiating too. Japan attacked Pearl Harbor after the talks failed.

4. Japan attacks Pearl Harbor · December 7, 1941

Japan attacked the U.S. Navy at Pearl Harbor in Hawaii. The surprise attack killed more than 2,400 Americans. The United States had not declared war on Japan before the attack.

History: The U.S. Congress declared war on Japan the next day. Germany and Italy then declared war on the United States. The U.S. fought in both Europe and the Pacific.
