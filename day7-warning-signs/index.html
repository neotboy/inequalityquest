Today's goal: Spot the tools dictators used to grab and keep power, and see how different dictators used the same tools.

Your job · about 10 minutes

After World War I, some leaders promised order, pride, and jobs. Then they used lies, fear, violence, and unfair laws to take control. These tools are warning signs (clues that a leader is becoming a dictator).

History note: One image shows Nazi anti-Jewish hate. The message is false and harmful. We study it to understand how people were targeted.

Four quick cases

For each case, choose the warning sign that the picture shows. Some warning signs may be used more than once.

1. Italy · 1920s–1930s · Mussolini

After World War I, Mussolini promised order and a stronger Italy. His followers attacked rivals. The king made him prime minister in 1922. Mussolini later banned rival parties.

Look at the picture: How is Mussolini shown?

2. Germany · May 1933 · A fire in Berlin

The Nazis burned books by writers they disliked. Nazi leaders also blamed Jewish people for Germany's problems. That was a lie used to turn people against a group.

Look at the picture: What is being destroyed?

3. Soviet Union · 1930s · Stalin

Stalin's government used posters and newspapers to praise him. Secret police arrested people called enemies. Some were sent away or killed.

Look at the picture: Who is shown as the hero?

4. Ethiopia · 1935 · Italian soldiers

In 1935, Mussolini sent Italy's army into Ethiopia, an independent African country. In 1939, Hitler's Germany did the same thing to Poland. Germany's attack on Poland began World War II in Europe.

Look at the picture: Whose soldiers are these, and whose land are they in?
