History note: One image shows Nazi anti-Jewish hate. The message is false and harmful. We study it to understand how people were targeted.

Five warning signs

1. Mussolini: one leader, one nation

After World War I, many Italians faced job problems and strikes. Mussolini promised order and a strong Italy. His Blackshirt followers attacked political rivals. In 1922, Italy's king made Mussolini prime minister. Mussolini then weakened elections and banned rival parties.

Look for: How does the image make Mussolini look?

2. Hitler: blame a group

Hitler and the Nazi Party blamed Jewish people for Germany's problems. This was a lie. After Hitler became chancellor in 1933, Nazi groups used threats and unfair rules to push Jewish people out of public life and businesses.

Look for: Who is being targeted? What does the sign tell people to do?

3. Hitler: burn books

In 1933, Nazi supporters burned books by writers they disliked. The Nazis wanted to control what people read and what ideas they heard. They also closed newspapers and punished critics.

Look for: What is being destroyed? What ideas might people lose?

4. Stalin: praise the leader, punish critics

After Lenin died, Stalin pushed rivals out and took control of the Soviet Union. His government used posters and newspapers to praise Stalin. Secret police arrested many people called enemies. Some were sent away or killed.

Look for: Who is shown as the hero? What might happen to people who disagree?

5. Dictators attack other countries

Dictators did not stop at controlling people at home. Mussolini's Italy invaded Ethiopia in 1935. Hitler's Germany invaded Poland in 1939. These attacks showed that dictators would use war to expand their power. Germany's invasion of Poland in 1939 began World War II in Europe.

Look for: Whose soldiers are in the picture? Where are they?
