Before the spark

Why was Europe tense?

These problems had been building for years.

4 causes
Nationalism

Strong pride in a people or country. Some people wanted to rule themselves.

Militarism

Countries built up their armies and navies and got ready to fight.

Alliances

Countries made deals to support one another. These deals could widen a war.

Imperialism

Powerful countries competed for land, trade, and colonies.

Remember: The killing began the crisis. These problems had already made Europe tense.

The Triple Alliance included Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy. Italy stayed neutral in 1914 and joined the Allies in 1915. France and Russia had a military alliance. Britain worked with them but had not promised to join every war. Britain’s decision in 1914 involved Belgium’s neutrality and Britain’s own security. Russia chose to support Serbia; no treaty forced it to fight for Serbia.

Dispatch complete

The crisis spread around the world.

The killing did not cause the war by itself. Europe was already tense. Leaders made choices that brought more countries into the war.

What to remember
Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia. Russia chose to support Serbia. Germany backed Austria-Hungary and declared war on Russia and France. Germany entered neutral Belgium, and Britain declared war on Germany. Empires and more countries brought the war to Africa, Asia, and the Middle East. The United States joined in 1917.
Long-term causes

Nationalism, militarism, alliances, and imperialism increased tension.

Immediate spark

Archduke Franz Ferdinand was assassinated in Sarajevo on June 28, 1914.

Alliance chain

Countries supported some partners and followed army plans. These choices helped widen the war.
